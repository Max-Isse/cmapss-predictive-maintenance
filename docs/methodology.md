# Methodology

Sections 1–2 cover the data and the features (phase 3), sections 3–4 the models and their uncertainty (phase 5),
section 5 the decision layer (phase 6). Evidence for every number below: [reports/features.md](../reports/features.md) (generated by
`cmapss gold`). Decisions: [decisions.md](decisions.md).

## 1. Data, unit of analysis and targets

- **Data.** NASA C-MAPSS FD001–FD004: simulated turbofan engines, one row per engine per operating cycle, three
  operating settings and 21 sensors. Training engines run until failure; test engines stop at an unknown point and
  NASA gives their true RUL at the last observed cycle. It is a simulation (see `data/README.md`): results describe
  this benchmark, not a real fleet.
- **Unit of analysis.** Engine × cycle in gold. A prediction is made at a cycle using that engine's history up to
  and including that cycle. Engines, not rows, are the independent units: splits and cross-validation group by
  engine (phase 5).
- **Regression target.** Remaining useful life `rul`, capped at 125 (`rul_capped`, piecewise linear; D-016).
  Above roughly 150–170 cycles before failure the sensors show no wear, so the uncapped target asks the model to
  predict something the data cannot show; the cap turns that into "healthy" and concentrates the fit on the
  degrading phase that matters for maintenance. Evaluation also reports the uncapped `rul`.
- **Classification target.** `fail_within_n` = 1 when `rul ≤ N`, with N = 30 cycles, the assumed maintenance
  lead time (D-017). It is the probability the decision layer uses.
- **Assumptions checked.** Engine cycles run 1..n without gaps (silver rule, D-011; none found). Test RUL covers
  every test engine (silver check). Six discrete operating conditions (every point within 0.005 standardised
  units of a centroid).
- **Where this can fail.** Two fault modes in FD003/FD004 move some sensors in opposite directions; a model
  assuming one direction per sensor would be wrong there. The RUL cap biases predictions for genuinely young
  engines towards 125 by design.

## 2. Features

All feature constants are fitted on training engines only and saved as a versioned spec; one function
(`features/build.py::build_features`) computes features for training, the replay and serving (D-013).

1. **Operating condition** (D-014): k-means (k = 6) on standardised settings, best of 10 seeds; each row gets the
   nearest centroid and the distance to it.
2. **Near-constant sensors removed** (D-013): sensors with at most 2 distinct values within every condition carry
   the condition, not health: 01, 05, 16, 18, 19 dropped; 16 kept sensors.
3. **Per-condition normalisation** (D-015): z-score each kept sensor with its condition's training mean and
   standard deviation. In FD002/FD004 this lifts the correlation of the strongest sensors with RUL from about 0 to
   −0.65…−0.69.
4. **Rolling statistics** (D-015): mean, standard deviation (PySpark window functions) and least-squares slope
   (Spark SQL `regr_slope`) of each normalised sensor over the last 5, 15 and 30 cycles; plus the cycle number.
   161 feature columns.
5. **Point-in-time correctness.** Windows are `RANGE BETWEEN w−1 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW` within the engine,
   and the spec constants are fitted once on the training set as a whole, like any scaler; test, replayed and
   served engines never contribute to them. (Training rows therefore see condition statistics that include later
   cycles of training engines: a standard fit-on-train effect with 8,000+ rows per condition, and the test set is
   unaffected.) Tested two
   ways: unit tests replace the future with garbage or cut it off and require identical features
   (`tests/test_features.py`), and every `cmapss gold` run recomputes features for all 1,416 engines cut at a
   random cycle and requires an exact match (134,007 rows, maximum difference 0).

**Not done, and why.** No smoothing filters (exponential or Savitzky–Golay) beyond rolling means: the window means
and slopes already reduce noise, and fewer feature families keep the model card readable. No condition-history
features (cycles spent in each condition): the per-condition normalisation removes the condition effect on each
reading; this can be revisited if FD002/FD004 errors are much higher than FD001's in phase 5.

## 3. Models and validation

- **Protocol** (D-020). Every fit and every choice uses 5-fold GroupKFold by engine on the 709 training engines;
  out-of-fold predictions are the validation evidence. The 707 official test engines are scored once by models
  refitted on all training engines. Results: [reports/model_comparison.md](../reports/model_comparison.md).
- **Why grouped folds.** Consecutive cycles of an engine are nearly identical; a row-level split would leak them
  across folds and flatter every metric. Tests check that folds never share an engine.
- **RUL** (D-021). Baselines: the mean capped RUL, and ridge regression on the same features (a linear
  degradation model). Main model: LightGBM on the capped RUL with fixed, conservative settings (no search: the
  gain over the baselines is the evidence, and a search would need nested CV to report honestly).
  Assumptions: the training fleet represents the test fleet (same simulation, same conditions); rows are
  conditionally informative given the last 30 cycles. Checked by the out-of-fold vs test comparison and by dataset.
- **Failure within 30 cycles** (D-021). Baselines: prevalence and logistic regression; LightGBM; LightGBM with an
  isotonic calibrator fitted on out-of-fold probabilities. Reported: PR-AUC (the positive class is rare),
  ROC-AUC, Brier score, calibration table and ECE, and recall at the threshold that gives 80% precision on the
  out-of-fold predictions.
- **Metrics on the official test set.** The standard C-MAPSS number is each test engine's last cycle against the NASA
  RUL (RMSE, MAE, PHM08 score, which penalises late predictions more). It mixes engines that are far from failure
  (true RUL above the cap, which the capped model cannot predict by design) with engines near failure, so the report
  also gives RMSE against the capped RUL, every test cycle, and segments by dataset and true-RUL band.
- **Where it fails.**
  - On the official last-cycle figure FD002 and FD004 look twice as bad as FD001 and FD003 (RMSE about 25 vs 13 against
    the NASA RUL). Against the capped RUL they are nearly level (12.5–12.9 vs 11.6–12.1): their test sets hold more
    engines whose true RUL is above the cap (22–27% vs 11–15%), which a capped model cannot predict. The headline
    C-MAPSS number partly measures the cap, not the model.
  - Predictions near failure are biased late (positive bias in the 31–60 band), the costly direction.
  - The precision of a fixed alert threshold does not transfer from training to test: test engines are truncated,
    so only 2.5% of test cycles are within 30 cycles of failure against about 14% in training, and the same threshold gives
    66% precision instead of 80%. Thresholds must therefore come from costs and base rates (phase 6), not from a
    precision target.

## 4. Uncertainty

- **Intervals** (D-022). Conformalized quantile regression: LightGBM 5% and 95% quantile models of the uncapped RUL,
  clipped to the cap, plus a conformal correction per band of predicted RUL computed on out-of-fold rows; 90%
  nominal coverage of the capped RUL within each predicted band.
- **Assumption and check.** Conformal coverage assumes exchangeable rows. Rows of one engine are dependent and the
  test engines are a different sample, so coverage is measured on the test engines, overall and by band.
- **Result.** Near failure (true RUL 0–30) coverage is 0.90 at a mean width of 24 cycles. Coverage is below nominal
  at true RUL 31–60 and 61–125 and above nominal for healthy engines, where most true values equal the cap.
- **Probabilities.** Isotonic calibration on out-of-fold probabilities; calibration is checked on the test engines
  (table and figure in the report). The decision layer uses these probabilities directly, so their calibration
  matters more than their ranking.

## 5. Decision layer

- **Rule** (D-024, D-025). Book a shop visit at the first cycle where the calibrated P(fail within N) reaches a
  threshold; the visit follows after the lead time L. The threshold minimises expected cost under the assumed costs
  (`business`), not a precision target. Results: [reports/maintenance_policy.md](../reports/maintenance_policy.md).
- **Evaluation.** Full engine lives of the 709 training engines with out-of-fold probabilities; thresholds and the
  fixed-interval baseline's removal cycles are chosen on four folds and scored on the fifth. Baselines: fixed
  interval (per dataset), run to failure; lower bound: perfect information.
- **Assumptions.** Costs are assumptions; one engine is one life (no repair or partial work); shop capacity is
  unlimited; the lead time is fixed. Sensitivity varies each cost, the horizon N and the lead time.
- **Where it fails.** The saving depends on the cost ratio: when an in-service failure costs little more than a
  planned visit (250 vs 200), fixed intervals collapse to run to failure and the model's edge shrinks to 13%.
  With N = L the decision happens where the classifier's probabilities are tiny and coarsely calibrated; a horizon
  longer than the lead time works better (D-025). The test engines confirm the rule fires mostly in time (246 of 249
  decisions inside the observed window), but 458 of 707 are censored, so the holdout check is partial.
